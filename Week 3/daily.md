# Week 3, Exercise 10

I experimented with the CurreChat DailyScrum and it worked correctly. In the "safe" setting the responses were made in an understandable way like "I'll help you with this issue after the daily, I have an hour". In the "blaming" setting the response was immediately "this was supposed to be an easy task". The difference showed how much the tone of a single reply changes whether a developer feels comfortable raising a blocker at all.

A good daily scrum shouldn't be with blaming people and the responses shouldn't be passive-aggresive. The team should be understanding and help as much as they can with any of the issues other developers encounter. When people are afraid of being blamed, they tend to hide problems until it's too late, which hurts the whole sprint. A safe environment keeps the daily short and useful, because blockers come out early and the team can agree on who will help and when.
